A tankless water heater works on a simple idea: instead of keeping 40 or 50 gallons hot around the clock, it heats water only while a tap is open. Flow through the unit trips a sensor, a powerful burner or electric element fires, and the water is heated as it passes through a compact heat exchanger. The upside is significant: no standby heat loss, hot water that does not run out during back-to-back showers, a wall-mounted unit that frees up floor space, and a service life that often runs close to two decades with maintenance. For many households in Warwick, RI, that makes tankless a smart upgrade.

Most tankless complaints trace back to poor sizing. A unit rated for plenty of flow on a spring day can struggle in winter, when incoming water is much colder and the heater has to raise it far more. So we size for your coldest months and your busiest moments, counting the fixtures that run together. We also confirm the gas line and meter can feed a burner that may be rated near 199,000 BTU, choose the correct venting for condensing or non-condensing equipment, and plan a condensate drain with a neutralizer where needed. For electric tankless, we check the panel first, because whole-home models can outstrip an older electrical service.

Every tankless install from Warwick Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, a clean gas connection with a sediment trap, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is the main enemy of a tankless heat exchanger. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. When to Call for Tankless Water Heater Installation

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Interest in a longer-lasting heater with replaceable parts
  • Adding a bathroom, soaking tub, or larger shower that needs more hot water
  • Showers turning lukewarm before everyone gets a turn
  • Limited floor space in a closet, garage, or utility room
  • Renovation work that makes gas line and vent changes easier to fit in
  • A far-off sink or bath where a point-of-use unit could help
  • Energy bills that reflect a tank heating water around the clock
  • A tank water heater that is ten years old or more and due for replacement

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Buying the right unit is the easy part. The other half is making sure your home can support it. Gas tankless burners can fire at close to 199,000 BTU, so the size of the gas line, the capacity of the meter, and the pressure at high fire all have to check out. Vent materials, lengths, and terminations are dictated by the manufacturer and local code, and condensing units add a condensate drain that should be neutralized. Electric whole-home units can pull more current than an older panel can deliver. Get any of these wrong, and the unit underperforms or becomes unsafe.

Our technicians handle the full picture. We size the unit by flow rate and winter temperature rise instead of guessing, confirm gas pressure with the burner at maximum, and install venting and condensate lines exactly to specification. Every install gets isolation valves for easy descaling and a properly piped relief valve. We also give honest advice about whether tankless suits your home at all. For some households, a high-efficiency tank or a heat pump water heater makes more sense, and you will hear that from us before you spend money on a system that does not match how you live.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Set the temperature around 120F for safety and efficiency
  • Do not cut power to a unit in a cold space, since its freeze protection needs electricity
  • Hard water shortens tankless life, so ask about scale protection
  • Make sure the outside vent and intake are never blocked
  • Flush the unit with a descaling solution annually to keep scale off the heat exchanger
  • Write down any error codes and call early rather than resetting repeatedly
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year
