Keeping Your Providence Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

Hot Water Headaches Providence Homeowners Face

Around Providence, water heater trouble tends to come from a handful of sources. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be a real factor: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the popping and rumbling you may hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Age is the other big factor. Many homes around here still have the original tank, and once a tank is past about ten years, the anode rod has usually been consumed and corrosion begins attacking the steel from the inside. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and heating elements and thermostats on electric ones.

Code requirements add another layer. Swapping out an older heater often means bringing the install up to current code: a thermal expansion tank if the water system is closed, a T&P discharge line piped to an approved spot, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, plus straps and a pan where required. Well water brings its own challenges, including extra sediment, iron staining, and the sulfur odor that occasionally develops when certain water chemistry reacts with a standard anode rod. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much cheaper when caught early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one. Call (401) 210-2665 and we'll take a look.
