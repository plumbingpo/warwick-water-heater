A gas water heater is a refreshingly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. When something goes wrong, it is usually at one of those points. In Warwick, RI, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Warwick Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Warwick call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • Rust-colored hot water or rust streaks on the tank

The Benefits of Professional Gas Water Heater Services

Two things make gas water heater work a poor DIY project: the parts involve fuel and combustion, and the symptoms overlap. A pilot outage might come from a worn thermocouple, a partly blocked pilot tube, lint on the flame arrestor screen, a downdraft, or an internal valve failure. A wrong guess there just wastes money. Guess wrong on a gas fitting or vent connection and the stakes are much higher, since a small leak or a spillage problem can put gas or carbon monoxide into the living space. Gas valve and heater replacements also typically call for a permit.

What a trained technician brings is certainty. We test instead of guessing, from thermocouple millivolt readings and burner flame checks to gas pressure at the valve and a draft test at the hood. We leak-test every connection, confirm the vent is carrying exhaust out of the house, and check CO before we leave. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Keep the area around the heater clear, especially of gasoline, paint, and solvents
  • Make sure the vent pipe stays connected, sloped upward, and free of rust
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Drain sediment from the bottom of the tank once or twice a year
