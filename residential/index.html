Upfront Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Warwick Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing on every job, handling everything from a burned-out heating element to a complete tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment settles on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod quietly dissolves, and the tank lining starts to rust from the inside with no visible warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, or moisture around the base, schedule an inspection sooner rather than later. An early diagnosis usually keeps a repairable issue from turning into a flooded floor.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is nearly always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap after the tank has already leaked. Our technicians know how local water conditions wear on tanks over time, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Annual Flushing

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Each maintenance visit from Warwick Water Heater is far more than opening the drain valve. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, tests the relief valve, inspects the burner, flue, and draft on gas models, checks elements and thermostats on electric models, and confirms a safe water temperature. Minor faults get caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and your water heater stays running clean and efficient.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and twice-yearly flushing where the water is hard. That's especially important in hard-water areas, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, makes the burner work harder, and shortens the life of the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and our technicians tailor the plan to your heater's age, your household's hot water demands, and your local water.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule pays off. A clean tank heats water faster with less noise, a healthy anode rod keeps rust from attacking the steel, and a working relief valve protects the whole house. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing a water heater even a few years early.

Right-Sized Hot Water

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, the number of gallons of hot water it can deliver in a busy hour, and for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can sustain at your incoming water temperature. We don't simply copy the size of the old unit. Instead, we look at how many people live in the home, the number of bathrooms, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely keeps up once kids are showering back to back, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water as long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line sizing, electrical service, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Most repair calls we get come down to a handful of recurring problems. The water never gets hot. The gas burner won't light, or it lights and shuts off. The hot water runs out fast, the tank pops every time the burner fires, or the tankless unit locks out with an error code. Add rusty water, sulfur smells, and dripping relief valves, and you have the full list. We fix every one of them, always targeting the component that actually failed.

The right repair depends entirely on what actually failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure problems get solved with an expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and we handle leak repairs at the drain valve and fittings before they spread. We also check the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. A tank leaking from the steel itself can't be patched, and we'll tell you so. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
